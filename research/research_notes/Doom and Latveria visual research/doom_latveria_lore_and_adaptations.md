## 1. Doctor Doom: character history, defining stories, personality and voice

### Takeaway
Doom is a 1962 Lee/Kirby creation whose defining traits are stable across six decades: an absolute monarch with a pompous, courtly manner, an honour code (he keeps his word), enormous vanity, and a self-image as a benevolent ruler who alone can save the world. Critics repeatedly describe his register as Shakespearean soliloquy rather than modern banter, which is the most useful single cue for invitation tone.

### Cited Findings
Creation and origin
- Created by Stan Lee (writer-editor) and Jack Kirby (artist/co-plotter); debut in Fantastic Four #5, cover-dated July 1962, on sale 10 April 1962. Full origin told in Fantastic Four Annual #2 (1964). — [Wikipedia: Doctor Doom](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Doctor_Doom)
- Lee on the name: "eloquent in its simplicity — magnificent in its implied menace." — [Wikipedia: Doctor Doom](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Doctor_Doom)
- Origin as summarised there: born in Latveria to a Romani family; mother Cynthia von Doom, a witch, killed when he was a baby; father Werner von Doom, a healer, died of exposure after fleeing with Victor. Victor won a scholarship to Empire State University, met Reed Richards, built a machine to reach his mother in Hell; a miscalculation caused an explosion that scarred his face. He then travelled to Tibet, where monks helped him forge the armor and an iron mask. — [Wikipedia: Doctor Doom](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Doctor_Doom)
- FF Annual #2 (1964) gives the Latverian birth, mother's death and Cynthia's magic practice; FF #278 (1985, John Byrne era) suggests the mask, put on straight from the forge before it cooled, did more damage than the explosion. — [BGR, Chris Smith, 30 Oct 2026](https://www.bgr.com/2301044/why-doctor-doom-wears-mask-marvel-comics-avengers-doomsday-meaning/)
- Byrne's version: the lab accident left only a small scar; Doom's arrogance turned it into real disfigurement when he donned the still-hot mask. Byrne's FF run began with #232 (March 1981); Doom first appears under Byrne in #236 (Nov 1981). — [Wikipedia: Doctor Doom](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Doctor_Doom)
- Doom is the son of a doctor (Werner) and a witch (Cynthia), which critics read as the source of his science-plus-sorcery duality; he has used black magic since FF #5. — [SlashFilm, Devin Meenan, 26 Jul 2025](https://www.slashfilm.com/1916991/what-fantastic-four-movies-get-wrong-marvel-doctor-doom)

Personality and voice
- Sean Howe: Doom was "insufferably pompous, but along with his haughty manner came a sort of honorable code; he always kept his word." John Byrne called Doom "a man of honor" despite his ruthlessness. — [Wikipedia: Doctor Doom](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Doctor_Doom)

### Inferences
- The verified 1969 lines show the exact rhetorical pattern useful for a "summons": third-person self-reference ("the rule of Doctor Doom"), possessive paternalism ("my beloved subjects"), a guest/prisoner ambiguity (hospitality that cannot be declined), and archaic insults for underlings ("jackanape").
- The honour-code point matters for tone: Doom's threats and promises are both binding. An invitation in his voice reads as a decree with guaranteed terms, not a request.
- The 2026 film dialogue ("Hell answers to me, for I am Doom"; "stolen lives") keeps the declarative, self-naming cadence, so the comics voice and the current film voice are compatible.

## 2. Monarch, scientist, sorcerer: how the three roles show up

### Takeaway
The three roles are explicit in both comics and official MCU material: sovereign of Latveria (regal tunic, cloak, throne, diplomatic immunity), inventor (powered armor, robots, time machine) and sorcerer (inherited from his mother; Tibetan training; later Sorcerer Supreme). Kirby's costume is itself the fusion: medieval-regal silhouette over futuristic armor.

### Cited Findings
- Kirby's Doom is described as a mix of classical and modern: a regal, tunic-based look plus futuristic armor and weapons. — [CBR, Cronin, 2021](https://www.cbr.com/marvel-doctor-doom-costume-changes-tunic/)
- Science: genius intellect; powered armor with strength, flight, lasers, force field, electric shock. Sorcery: trained by Tibetan monks, magic enhanced via Morgan le Fay; energy projection, dimensional travel, summoning demons. — [Wikipedia: Doctor Doom](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Doctor_Doom)
- Monarchy: Latveria is an absolute monarchy with no legislature. — [Wikipedia: Latveria](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Latveria)
- Official MCU book From Endgame to Doomsday: A Marvel Studios Visual Dictionary describes Doom as a mysterious, enigmatic monarch and master of sorcery and technology whose intellect rivals Reed Richards'. — [Comic Basics, Arthur S. Poe, 7 Nov 2026](https://www.comicbasics.com/?p=202053)
- MCU characterisation per Wikipedia: a powerful being who wields dark magic and has lost a child; in the SDCC footage he first appears seated on a throne, uses magic on Thor and raises Sentinels. — [Wikipedia: Avengers: Doomsday](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Avengers:_Doomsday); [Variety Australia, 26 Jul 2026](https://au.variety.com/2026/film/news/avengers-doomsday-new-trailer-comic-con-38885/)
- 2025's One World Under Doom unites the roles at maximum scale: Sorcerer Supreme and world emperor. — [Wikipedia: Doctor Doom](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Doctor_Doom)

### Inferences
- Visual shorthand per role: monarch = throne, cloak, clasps, banners, portraits; scientist = armor, monitors, robots, laboratory; sorcerer = hood, candlelit ritual spaces, runes. A "royal summons" leans on the first set, with the other two as undertones.

### Gaps
- No creator interview was found that explicitly discusses the three-role structure as a design principle.

## 3. Armor, mask, hood, cloak, tunic: canonical elements, evolution, symbolism, the green question

### Takeaway
The canonical look is remarkably stable: riveted iron mask, grey steel armor, green hooded cloak and tunic, two round cloak clasps joined by a chain or cord, belt and gauntlets. Kirby said the armor and hood exist because Doom was conceived as Death. No documented creator reason for the green colour was found.

### Cited Findings
Canonical elements
- 1962 look as described by one retrospective: a riveted iron helmet, a hard symmetrical mask, a hooded green cloak, and a chestplate with medieval-armorer's buckles; green and metal grey stayed dominant for decades. — [MyComicsCollection, Doom key issues (search snippet)](https://mycomicscollection.com/en/blog/doctor-doom-comics-history/)
- The appearance has stayed consistent: steel mask, armor, green hooded cloak, royal tunic. — [search snippet aggregated from several results incl. Den of Geek](https://www.denofgeek.com/?p=979218)
- The 2026 film's primary costume reproduces the same vocabulary: iron mask, green hooded cape and green tunic, two large brooches strung together with a chain, plated chestpiece, armored shoulders, metal gloves and boots; described as "basic Jack Kirby-esque." — [SlashFilm, Devin Meenan, 14 Aug 2026](https://www.slashfilm.com/2237350/disney-d23-doctor-doom-avengers-doomsday-second-costume-photos/)

Creator statements and symbolism
- Kirby (1987): "Dr. Doom was the classic conception of Death"; "It was the reason for the armor and the hood." — [Wikipedia: Doctor Doom](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Doctor_Doom)
- Kirby's view of the face: only "a tiny scar on his cheek", so Doom hides "not from the world, but from himself"; in 1992 Kirby said "He gets a cut on his chin!" and that the scar "grows so large that it affects his entire brain." — [Wikipedia: Doctor Doom](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Doctor_Doom)
- Lee's and Byrne's versions make the disfigurement real and self-inflicted through arrogance (the hot mask). — [Wikipedia: Doctor Doom](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Doctor_Doom); [BGR](https://www.bgr.com/2301044/why-doctor-doom-wears-mask-marvel-comics-avengers-doomsday-meaning/)
- Critical readings: the mask represents Doom refusing responsibility for his scars and blaming others (BGR); the mask is his self-made identity and isolation and he should never be seen without it (SlashFilm). — [BGR](https://www.bgr.com/2301044/why-doctor-doom-wears-mask-marvel-comics-avengers-doomsday-meaning/); [SlashFilm 2025](https://www.slashfilm.com/1916991/what-fantastic-four-movies-get-wrong-marvel-doctor-doom)
- Kaminski notes the design's strong similarity to Darth Vader and suggests Doom may have influenced Vader. — [Wikipedia: Doctor Doom](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Doctor_Doom)

Evolution (documented departures; all reverted to the classic tunic look)
- Secret Wars #10 (1984–85; Shooter/Zeck/Beatty): futuristic armor at Mattel's request; classic look back by FF #300 (Stern/Buscema). — [CBR, Cronin](https://www.cbr.com/marvel-doctor-doom-costume-changes-tunic/); [CBR on the action-figure costume (search snippet)](https://www.cbr.com/doctor-doom-secret-wars-action-figure-costume/)
- Walter Simonson, FF #350: tunic-less/altered tunic; reverted by FF #361 (DeFalco/Ryan/Bulanadi). FF #375: spiked "Watcher absorption" armor; reverted by #381. — [CBR, Cronin](https://www.cbr.com/marvel-doctor-doom-costume-changes-tunic/)
- "Unthinkable" (Waid/Wieringo): leather armor made from Valeria's skin; classic armor and tunic back by FF #536 (Straczynski/McKone). — [CBR, Cronin](https://www.cbr.com/marvel-doctor-doom-costume-changes-tunic/)
- Infamous Iron Man (Bendis/Maleev): no armor/Iron Man suit; classic look restored in Dan Slott's Fantastic Four (#5, Aaron Kuder). — [CBR, Cronin](https://www.cbr.com/marvel-doctor-doom-costume-changes-tunic/)
- Secret Wars (2015) God Emperor Doom: described as all-white armor by one list and golden by another (sources conflict). — [CBR best Doom costumes (search snippet)](https://www.cbr.com/marvel-comics-doctor-doom-best-costumes/)
- Modern painterly references: a Yahoo piece says the MCU mask and hood resemble modern comic art of Doom "especially from artists like Alex Ross and Adi Granov." — [Yahoo Entertainment (search snippet)](https://www.yahoo.com/entertainment/movies/articles/doctor-doom-look-avengers-doomsday-211830953.html)
- Mignola's Triumph and Torment is noted for heavy shadows and sharp line work. — [search snippet; SuperHeroHype](https://www.superherohype.com/features/630273-best-doctor-doom-comics)

The green question
- Wikipedia's Doom article gives no reason for the green; two targeted searches (including colourist Stan Goldberg and Silver Age colour conventions) found no documented statement. — [Wikipedia: Doctor Doom](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Doctor_Doom)

### Inferences
- Because every redesign has reverted, the tunic-cloak-clasps-mask set is the safe "canon" silhouette; anything else reads as a specific story variant.
- Kirby's "Death" statement licenses reading the hooded cloak as a reaper's cowl crossed with a monarch's mantle; the green then functions as a livery colour rather than a symbol with a stated meaning.
- The MCU has chosen the Kirby-classic vocabulary, so in late 2026 the comics look and the mainstream-recognisable film look coincide.

### Gaps
- No documented creator/colourist reason for green was found. The common explanation that Silver Age villains were coloured in secondary colours (green/purple) is fan/critic folklore that I could not source; treat as unverified.
- Specific contributions of John Byrne, Mike Mignola, Alex Ross, Esad Ribić and Salvador Larroca to the costume's rendering (e.g. Byrne's hot-mask scene visuals, Ribić's white God Emperor design, Larroca's metallic realism) could not be sourced in detail; Marvel Database's "Doctor Doom's Armor" page exists but was inaccessible. — [Marvel Database: Doctor Doom's Armor (not accessed)](https://marvel.fandom.com/wiki/Doctor_Doom's_Armor)
- Canonical detail list from the brief (rectangular mouth grille, holster/pistol, gold colour of clasps) is not itemised in any source read; the SYFY piece does not call the clasps gold. FF #84 does feature Doom's "ray-pistol." — [50 Year Old Comics](https://50yearoldcomics.com/2018/12/22/fantastic-four-84-march-1969/)

## 4. Power, authority and technology

### Takeaway
Doom's authority is legal as well as physical: he is a head of state with diplomatic immunity and embassies, and he polices his own country with Doombots and pervasive hidden surveillance. His signature devices are the time platform (from his first appearance) and robot doubles.

### Cited Findings
- Time machine introduced in FF #5 (1962) and central to Marvel's time-travel plots. Doombots maintain order in Latveria. Diplomatic immunity as head of state shields him from prosecution abroad. Ovoid mind-transfer technique. — [Wikipedia: Doctor Doom](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Doctor_Doom)
- Hidden monitoring devices cover the country; hidden weapons reportedly prevent people leaving without his consent. — [Wikipedia: Latveria](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Latveria)
- "Visual, audible and motion sensors honeycomb Castle Doom and Latveria." — [MarvelDirectory (unofficial reference; search snippet)](https://marveldirectory.com/miscellaneous/castledoom.htm)
- Latverian Embassy in New York: first clear Latverian flag flies outside it in Incredible Hulk #143 (1971); embassy is Doom's base in Fantastic Four: World's Greatest Heroes (2006) and appears in the games The Incredible Hulk (2008) and Marvel: Avengers Alliance. — [Frogoat blog](https://frogoat.blogspot.com/search/label/flag); [Wikipedia: Doctor Doom in other media](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Doctor_Doom_in_other_media); [Wikipedia: Latveria](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Latveria)
- Economy depends largely on Doom's high-tech inventions; he refuses the EU and the euro. Lore summaries describe Latveria as known for robotics, advanced weaponry and tech exports. — [Wikipedia: Latveria](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Latveria); [The Direct](https://thedirect.com/article/marvel-video-doctor-doom-latveria)
- After the S.H.I.E.L.D. invasion (Dark Avengers era) Doom rebuilt Latveria using time-travel technology. — [Wikipedia: Latveria](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Latveria)

### Inferences
- "Diplomatic" trappings (embassy, seal, protocol, sealed borders) are canon-consistent framing devices for a summons: Doom acts through the forms of statecraft.

### Gaps
- Specific issue citations for diplomatic immunity and for the first Doombot were not retrieved.

## 6. Latveria: fictional geography

### Takeaway
Latveria is a tiny landlocked monarchy first shown in FF Annual #2 (1964), now officially placed in the Banat region where Hungary, Romania and Serbia meet, ringed by the Carpathians, with fictional Symkaria to the south. Capital: Doomstadt (formerly Haasenstadt). Detailed almanac numbers exist in Marvel handbooks but could not be verified this session.

### Cited Findings
- First appearance FF Annual #2 (July 1964), Lee and Kirby. Located in Southeastern Europe, "supposedly in the Banat region," surrounded by the Carpathian Mountains; borders Symkaria (Silver Sable's country) to the south; formed from land annexed from southern Hungary centuries ago and possibly from Serbia; Wikipedia gives approximate coordinates 46°N, 20.6°E. — [Wikipedia: Latveria](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Latveria)
- Screen Rant (cited in Wikipedia) notes the Banat placement was identified in 2020. — [Wikipedia: Latveria](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Latveria)
- Earlier vagueness: the 1964 story places it "nestling in the heart of the Bavarian alps" (per Mark Hibbett), and FF #84 (1969) has the FF enter from "Communist-occupied Central Europe." — [Wikipedia: Latveria](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Latveria); [50 Year Old Comics](https://50yearoldcomics.com/2018/12/22/fantastic-four-84-march-1969/)
- Capital Doomstadt, formerly Haasenstadt (spelled "Hassenstadt" in some sources and in the 2007 film). — [MarvelDirectory](https://marveldirectory.com/miscellaneous/castledoom.htm); [Wikipedia: Latveria](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Latveria)
- Population: fan wikis give about 500,000, noting the regime is secretive about census data. — [Superheroes fandom wiki (search snippet)](https://superheroes.fandom.com/wiki/Latveria?oldid=7290); [World's Biggest Comic Database wiki (search snippet)](https://worldsbiggestcomicdatabase.shoutwiki.com/wiki/Latveria)
- Doomwood Forest is listed among Doomstadt-area locations by a wiki. — [search snippet, same wikis](https://worldsbiggestcomicdatabase.shoutwiki.com/wiki/Latveria)
- Place names on an unconfirmed-origin "Avengers: Doomsday" Latveria map (first shared by the account MCU Film News; origin NOT confirmed): Doomstadt (south, starred), Castle Von Doom beside it, Doomwood Forest (west), Mt. Victorum (north, with a river), Doom Falls, Doomsburg, Doomton, Citadel of Doom, Lanzarini Temple, Werner Academy, and a peak named something like "Mt. Sorcista." The article says many names match comics locations. — [Comic Basics, 7 Nov 2026](https://www.comicbasics.com/?p=202053)
- Marvel Atlas #1 (Nov 2007; Michael Hoskin et al.) contains a Latveria entry (pp. 36–37 cover the region incl. Symkaria). — [Frogoat blog](https://frogoat.blogspot.com/search/label/flag); [Symkaria wiki mirror (search snippet)](https://bafybeiemxf5abjwjbikoz4mc3a3dla6ual3jsgpdr4cjr3oz3evfyavhwq.ipfs.4everland.io/wiki/Symkaria.html)

### Inferences
- "Bavarian Alps" (1964) versus "Banat/Carpathians" (current) is a real canon drift; the visual tradition (Alpine/Germanic fairy-tale village) comes from the earlier placement, the political geography from the later one.

## 7. Castle Doom and Doomstadt: how they are described and drawn

### Takeaway
The consistent picture is a storybook Central European village (peasant cottages, caravans, a square, a café) beneath a 16th-century, 110-room castle, with high technology and robots concealed inside the medieval shell. The uneasy cheerfulness of the villagers is part of the look.

### Cited Findings
- Castle Doom: built in the 16th century by the Latverian nobleman Count Sabbat; 110 rooms; overlooks Doomstadt; taken by Doom after usurping King Vladimir, with a brief interruption under Prince Zorba. — [MarvelDirectory (unofficial)](https://marveldirectory.com/miscellaneous/castledoom.htm)

- FF #84–87 (1969): a seemingly benign isolated village under the castle; villagers "friendly, but extremely jumpy"; robots field-tested on the village; borrowed surface elements of The Prisoner (benign village, hidden masters, unsettling technology). — [50 Year Old Comics](https://50yearoldcomics.com/2018/12/22/fantastic-four-84-march-1969/)

## 8. Royal symbolism, heraldry and insignia

### Takeaway
There is no single canonical Latverian flag or coat of arms. Comics have used at least four distinct motifs (a stylised "D", an eagle, an eagle on a sceptre, and a gauntlet gripping lightning bolts), and the only colour scheme explicitly documented in the source read is red with a black eagle (1971). The best documentation is a fan vexillology blog; treat it as secondary.

### Cited Findings
All from [Frogoat blog, "flag" label](https://frogoat.blogspot.com/search/label/flag) (fan research crediting Doom2099.com; page carries scans but images were not inspected) unless noted:
- FF Annual #2 (1964): first regal symbols; recurring motifs are a stylised capital "D" and an eagle emblem.
- FF #85–87 (1969): assorted banners; an eagle on a sceptre; the "D" returns. The blogger suggests the "D" is Victor's personal mark rather than a national one.
- Incredible Hulk #143 (1971): first clear national flag, outside the New York embassy: red field with a black eagle-like device at centre (colours inverted in a night scene).
- Emperor Doom (1987): stylised "D" insignia on objects once Doom rules Earth.
- Triumph and Torment (1989): eagle-and-sceptre design returns.
- Fantastic Four vol. 2 #4 (Heroes Reborn era): several flags including "D" and eagle designs.
- Doom: The Emperor Returns #1–2: first version of what the blogger calls the most iconic Latverian flag (possibly the sovereign's standard rather than the nation's). Reused in Secret War #5, Penance: Relentless #4, Books of Doom #6, Captain America vol. 5 #23, and in Fortnite's "Absolute Doom" (Aug 2024).
- Marvel Atlas #1 (2007): a markedly different flag, an armoured gauntlet grasping lightning bolts; not seen reused.
- A file named Flag_of_Latveria.png is used on wikis; Wikipedia's infobox shows a flag with no description. — [Wikipedia: Latveria](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Latveria)
- MCU-adjacent: the unconfirmed-origin film map has "Latveria" in gothic lettering and a green flag at its centre, plus a hooded figure marking Doomstadt; the Jeep ad's tower reportedly bears a red diamond symbol. — [Comic Basics](https://www.comicbasics.com/?p=202053); [GeekTyrant (search snippet)](https://geektyrant.com/news/first-look-at-doctor-dooms-tower-and-latveria-revealed-in-avengers-doomsday-jeep-commercial)
- MCU costume insignia: Doom's two circular cloak clasps carry what look like Captain Marvel's emblem and a Mjolnir device; fans theorise they are trophies; Marvel has not confirmed. The SDCC display also showed a yellow ribbon-like band on the left arm. — [SYFY WIRE, Josh Weiss, 20 Aug 2026](https://www.syfy.com/syfy-wire/doctor-doom-cloak-fasteners-in-avengers-doomsday-explored); [Comic Basics, costume reveals](https://www.comicbasics.com/every-doctor-doom-costume-reveal-from-avengers-doomsday-so-far-and-what-it-could-mean/)
- A search snippet mentions "Latveria logo leaks" around CinemaCon 2026; no detail and no confirmation. — [search snippet, TBreak](https://tbreak.com/first-real-avengers-doomsday-trailer-here/)

### Inferences
- Because canon is plural, an original crest for an invitation can legitimately combine the recurring motifs (eagle, sceptre, "D", gauntlet) without contradicting any authoritative design. The "D" reads as Doom's personal cypher (like a royal monogram), the eagle as the state.
- Doom's mask-as-emblem is common in merchandise and fan work, but I found no source establishing it as in-universe Latverian heraldry.

### Gaps
- The exact appearance and colours of the "iconic" Emperor Returns flag were not described in the text retrieved, and no image was inspected. Green/white/black as national colours could not be verified.
- Official Handbook of the Marvel Universe entries were not accessible; whether OHOTMU prints a flag is unverified.
- No film (2005/2007/2015) heraldry descriptions were found.

### Takeaway
Verified: the Latverian franc, absolute rule, pervasive surveillance, closed borders, and citizens who are outwardly content and inwardly afraid. An anthem, motto, official languages and the holiday list could not be verified because the handbook-derived sources were inaccessible.

## 16. Which interpretation best supports a royal, gothic, cinematic "summons from Latveria"

### Takeaway
The strongest foundation is the classic comics Doom (Kirby's 1969 Latveria for world texture, Byrne for honour and courtliness, Mignola's Triumph and Torment for gothic atmosphere), which is also what the 2026 film has adopted visually. This is an inference from the findings above, not a sourced verdict.

### Cited Findings
- The MCU's primary costume is explicitly "Jack Kirby-esque" (green hooded cape and tunic, chained brooches, iron mask). — [SlashFilm](https://www.slashfilm.com/2237350/disney-d23-doctor-doom-avengers-doomsday-second-costume-photos/)
- Critics argue Doom works when he is a self-made monarch standing apart and played as a Shakespearean figure; earlier films failed by making him a CEO or programmer. — [SlashFilm 2025](https://www.slashfilm.com/1916991/what-fantastic-four-movies-get-wrong-marvel-doctor-doom)
- Canon heraldry is plural (eagle, sceptre, "D", gauntlet), leaving room for an original crest. — [Frogoat blog](https://frogoat.blogspot.com/search/label/flag)
- The film is unreleased until 18 Dec 2026 and its Latveria interiors are largely unseen. — [Wikipedia: Avengers: Doomsday](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Avengers:_Doomsday)

### Inferences
- Recommended base: comics-classic Doom and Latveria. Reasons: maximum canon depth for court, protocol and voice; compatible with the current film look; avoids dependence on unreleased or leaked film material; gothic and royal registers are native to it.
- Alternative A, MCU-forward (Russo/Downey): highest recognition in Nov–Dec 2026 and a ready-made cinematic palette (snowy mountains, tower, throne, ornate clasps). Risks: little confirmed Latveria imagery, heavy leak/AI contamination, and details may change on release.
- Alternative B, God Emperor (Secret Wars 2015) or Emperor of the World (2025): more imperial and theological than royal-gothic; white/gold palette departs from green-and-iron; suits a "decree to all nations" framing more than a personal summons.
- Alternative C, Fortnite/games Latveria: most complete explorable reference for castle, village, forge and flag, but stylised and bright; useful as layout reference, weak for mood.
- Alternative D, official parody tone ("What The--?!" tourism film): shows Marvel itself plays Doom's hospitality for dark comedy; an option if the invitation wants wit rather than dread.
- Tone anchors with sourced support: third-person self-naming, binding word, paternal contempt, guests who are also captives, everything named after Doom, an empty chair at other people's tables.

- No source directly evaluates Doom interpretations for event or invitation design; the ranking is the researcher's judgment.
