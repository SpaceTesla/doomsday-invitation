# Seven images describe one room well

The supplied reference library covers one costume and one building interior, and almost nothing else. Six of its seven images depict a single film design, the *Avengers: Doomsday* Doctor Doom, so the set is unusually coherent: a near-black base, green carried by light and air instead of by surfaces, gold as the colour of light, cold steel as the neutral, and compositions built on symmetry, height and a hidden face. One file, `doom-throme-wide.png`, is strong enough to anchor setting, palette and lighting for the whole project; the rest contribute a single thing each (mood, costume detail, silhouette, a phone-shaped layout, ornament, proportions). Two findings correct the brief's assumptions. The film's architecture is Art Nouveau and Art Deco at Gothic scale, with no pointed arch or rib vault anywhere, and `doom-castle.webp` is an interior tapestry hall, not a castle exterior, so the library contains no view of Latveria as a place. Everything outside the throne room (exteriors, heraldry, typography, documents, high-resolution texture, any comic-book interpretation) is missing and has to come from the external research in RESEARCH.md. All seven files are reference only: none has provenance or a licence, two carry studio or third-party marks, all depict or derive from studio-owned designs, and none is large or clean enough to be an asset.

## How this analysis was made

All seven files under `references\` were opened and viewed individually by the image researcher on 15 November 2026. The author of this synthesis did not reopen them; every description below is relayed from that researcher's notes. Nothing in `references\` was modified. The folder holds three subfolders, `character`, `environment` and `inspiration`, and **`references\inspiration\` exists but is empty**. No sidecar text, credit or licence file accompanies any image.

Four evidence labels are used throughout.

| Label | Meaning |
|---|---|
| **Observed** | Seen directly in the image or an enlarged crop of it |
| **Measured** | Computed from the file. For colour: the image was decoded read-only, reduced to 96 × 96 pixels, binned at 3 bits per channel, and the mean colour of each bin reported. Shares are approximate and describe area, not importance |
| **By eye** | A hex value estimated visually for a small accent the binning did not isolate; accurate to a few percent per channel |
| **Inferred** | The image researcher's interpretation. Marked as such, or set in a blockquote |

Passages headed **Synthesis (this report)** are connections made while combining the image notes with the other research; they are not the image researcher's findings.

## Image 1: `doom-1.png`, a poster figure rising out of green smoke

**What is depicted (observed).** A frontal standing figure seen from slightly below, arms at the sides with gauntleted fists, legs disappearing into smoke at the shins. The hood is deep and very dark green, and the face is almost entirely in shadow: only a faint metallic highlight down the nose and cheek reads as a mask, and no eyes are visible. Two round disc clasps sit at the collarbones, joined by a chain. The chest is a grid of small square tiles with engraved centres. There is a layered dark pauldron on the viewer's right shoulder, an olive-yellow cloth band around that upper arm, ridged forearm guards, a multi-strap belt and a split tunic skirt. The cape flares outward on both sides into a broad dark triangle that fills the lower half of the frame. The image carries a white "glamsham" watermark, a small Marvel Studios logo, and the tagline "12.18.26 IS DOOMSDAY" in a wide-tracked, squared, extended sans-serif with the Avengers "A" emblem standing in for a letter.

**Lighting and shadow (observed).** The smoke is the light source, brightest behind and to the left, so the figure is in effect backlit. A narrow warm gold rim traces the viewer's left edge. The face is deliberately underexposed. Shadows are soft; smoke covers the whole frame and swallows the feet. The image has no true highlights (0% of pixels above brightness 180, measured).

**Materials and textures (observed, limited by resolution).** Matte heavy cloth, dark satin-finish plate, slightly raised tile relief, a cloth arm wrap. Weave, rivets and engraving cannot be resolved at 388 pixels.

**Emotional atmosphere.** Menace, arrival, inevitability. The hidden face makes it read as an apparition, not a person.

**Potential applications.** A mood reference for a moment in which a figure emerges from fog; a silhouette reference (hood peak, broad shoulders, triangular cape); a source for the olive-smoke end of the palette; a record of how the film's marketing letterspaces its date line.

**Specifications, provenance and limits.** 388 × 515 pixels. Third-party watermark, Marvel Studios and Avengers marks, a real release date. The watermark indicates it was saved from an entertainment news outlet, not a studio source (inferred). Whether it is an official one-sheet or a fan-made poster is **unknown**; it is not the official teaser poster the press describe, which shows Doom "with his back to the camera, looking at an ornate tapestry on a wall" ([Nerdist](https://nerdist.com/article/new-avengers-doomsday-poster-hints-at-doctor-doom-tragic-history/)). Its date matches the film's reported release date ([Den of Geek](https://www.denofgeek.com/movies/avengers-doomsday-trailer-breakdown-doctor-doom-x-men-captain-america-returns/)). Nothing visible proves or rules out AI generation. Mask, leg and boot design are unreadable. It depicts a character design owned by Marvel and Disney. Reference only.

**Relevance to the direction.** The strongest "imposing apparition" image in the set and the best example of the hidden face, which serves "mysterious" directly. Useful for atmosphere and silhouette only.

The **cuirass** is a grid of square tiles, each embossed with a square inside a rotated square, separated by narrow hatched bands; about eight columns and four rows are visible. Two glass styles share one room: flowing organic leading in the main windows and rigid stacked geometric motifs in the side windows. The circular floor inlay functions as a seal or glyph. **No pointed arches, rib vaults, gargoyles, banners or heraldry are visible.**

**Composition and framing (observed).** One-point symmetry; the figure is small and exactly centred, about 45% down. The steps form a pyramid that leads the eye to the throne. The outer 25% on each side is dark, broken only by narrow glass strips and the lamps, and the bottom 25% is dark floor with the glyph at its centre; the upper centre is bright and busy. A centred phone-shaped crop would be only about 686 × 1220 pixels.

**Emotional atmosphere.** Awe, stillness, sovereignty, with an undercurrent of sorrow. It feels like a cathedral that has become a seat of power.

**Potential applications.** The primary mood and layout reference for a hero or arrival scene; the primary palette source (near-black, olive, sage, amber, one acid-green accent); a reference for the direction and layering of light shafts, the treatment of a floor glyph, and window design.

**Specifications, provenance and limits.** 2880 × 1220 pixels at 192 dpi with a thin black strip along the top edge; softer than its pixel count because it is a capture of video. The researcher infers it is very likely the opening shot of the official trailer: "We open on Downey's Doom on a throne, in all likelihood within Castle Doomstadt in Latveria... Lines carved into the ground glow with green mystical energy, sketching out what looks like a sizable glyph" ([eGamers](https://egamers.io/avengers-doomsday-7-things-the-new-d23-trailer-reveals-about-doctor-doom/)). The "Castle Doomstadt" identification is that outlet's guess. The glyph's full design and the meaning of the plaque emblems cannot be read, and the frame could not be verified as unaltered. Film frame, studio-owned. Reference only.

**Relevance to the direction.** It satisfies royal, mysterious, imposing and cinematic at once, and it is the clearest statement of the film's environment language.

## Six of seven images share one visual language

Because six images depict one film design, the recurring patterns are strong, and they are also all drawn from a single source.

**Darkness is the base (measured).** The darkest colour bin is the largest in every image, from 16.3% to 74.2%, and above 32% in six of seven. Mean brightness is below 70 out of 255 in six of seven.

**Green is light and air, not surface (observed and measured).** In four images green is carried by smoke, haze, glass and glow; the cloak itself measures close to black-green (#13261B to #1C2423). Atmospheric greens sit at roughly 30 to 46% saturation. Saturated green appears only in the floor energy, the poster tagline and the display lighting of `doom-2.png`.

**Gold and amber are the colour of light (observed).** Rim light in `doom-1.png`, lamps and spotlight in the tapestry hall, step edges and glass in both throne images.

**Steel is the neutral (observed).** The mask and armour are silver to gunmetal in every character image, with rivet lines as the signature detail.

**A fixed costume kit (observed in all four character images).** Deep hood, riveted plate mask, two disc clasps with a chain, tile cuirass with square or diamond motifs, chainmail, oversized forearm guards. The twin medallions are asymmetric: a star or sunburst on one side, a square-cross on the other.

**A small geometric vocabulary (observed).** Concentric squares and diamonds on the cuirass; circles in the medallions, lamp bowls, floor glyph and tapestry sphere; long vertical lines in the throne back, windows and cloak.

**Backlight, silhouette and haze (observed).** Three images put the light behind the figure; all three environments show light shafts.

**The hidden face (observed).** The face is unreadable in `doom-1.png` and in all three environment images.

**Central symmetry and a small figure in a vast space (observed).** Four images are centred and symmetrical; all three environments make the figure tiny. Repeated props (the bowl lamps, the curved green glass) tie the spaces together.

> **Inferred (image researcher).** A workable palette skeleton from the measurements: base black #0B0F0C to #10110D; shadow green #13261B to #192E24; haze green #324A2E to #526C4A; sage light #95A772 to #CDC890; amber and gold #8F6B34 to #CBA838; steel #6C7070 to #ABB1B1; and an energy green near #5ED12F as an accent only. The recurring rule appears to be "one saturated accent, tiny area", and following it is probably what will make original artwork feel related to the references. The square, diamond and circle motifs are simple enough to be redrawn as original ornament without copying a specific film asset. Every pattern here derives from one film's design; the library offers no independent check against any other interpretation.

## Eight conflicts, each reconcilable by giving things fixed roles

The conflicts in the set are mostly about grade and register, not subject. The reconciliations below are the image researcher's design reasoning; no reference in the library demonstrates the reconciled result.

| Conflict (observed or measured) | How it could be reconciled (inferred) |
|---|---|
| **Three different greens.** Muted olive and teal haze in the poster and wide frame; saturated emerald with lime, amber and blue in `doom-2.png`; almost no green in `doom-3.png` | Assign three roles and never swap them. Cloth green: almost black, large areas, no glow. Atmosphere green: muted olive-sage, for haze and glass. Energy green: saturated, only for small self-lit elements. Discard `doom-2.png` as a colour source and keep it for detail |
| **Warm against cool.** The portrait throne room is dominated by yellow and gold; the wide frame shows the same room in cool sage and teal; the tapestry hall is amber-warm; `doom-3.png` is cold grey | Make temperature mean something. Warm gold for light from windows, lamps and whatever the guest is invited toward; cool green-teal for shadow, depth and the supernatural. Use the wide frame's balance (mostly cool and dark with one warm shaft) as the default and reserve the fully warm look for one deliberate moment |
| **"Gothic" against what the film shows.** The environments have curved Art Nouveau leading, Art Deco stepped geometry and plain monumental piers. No pointed arch, rib vault or carved Gothic detail appears in any image | Take Gothic for proportion and mood (extreme height, narrow tall windows, cathedral symmetry, darkness) and Nouveau and Deco for line (flowing leading, stepped geometry). Avoid adding stock Gothic elements the references do not support, such as gargoyles, bats and blackletter everywhere, or the result reads as a generic haunted castle |
| **Six rendering styles in seven images.** Photoreal film frames, a painterly regrade, an airbrushed digital painting, a photograph of a physical costume, a photograph of printed art, a compressed poster | Pick one baseline. Use the photoreal look of the wide frame as the grade and texture standard for environments; use the other images only for the information they hold; unify new artwork with one shared grade, grain and haze |
| **Medieval craft against technology.** Chainmail, tapestry, wool and stone-bowl lamps sit beside machined perforated forearm guards, a glowing energy glyph and the poster's extended technical sans-serif | Let technology show only as light. Keep visible surfaces hand-made (metal, cloth, stone, glass) and express anything futuristic as glow, not as panels, circuitry or interface chrome. Do not adopt the poster's sans as the main voice; if echoed, limit it to numerals or dates |
| **Menace against mourning.** Confrontation in three character images; grief and stillness in the tapestry hall and both throne images | Sequence them instead of blending them. A summons leads with the imposing register; the quiet, sorrowful register can pace the moments between, adding depth without softening the host |
| **Face visible against hidden.** Human eyes show in `doom-3.png` and `doom-4.jpg`; the face is hidden in the poster and the environments; the eye openings are empty in `doom-2.png` | Keep it hidden. The shadowed hood serves "mysterious" better than visible eyes |
| **Format.** Five portrait files and two ultrawide; the only high-resolution environment is ultrawide, and the only portrait environment is 736 pixels wide | Design two compositions, not one crop: the wide frame's layout for landscape and the portrait throne room's layout for phones. Original artwork is needed for both, because neither reference has the resolution or the rights to be used directly |

The architecture finding deserves emphasis because it changes what "gothic" can mean in the next phase. The image researcher's reading is that the film mixes Art Nouveau main windows and Art Deco side windows and throne geometry inside a Gothic-scale volume, and that "that mix, not pure Gothic, is the film's actual architectural language."

## The images against the external research

This section is **synthesis (this report)** throughout: it compares the image researcher's observations with the lore, real-world, design and motion research in RESEARCH.md. None of these comparisons was made by the original researchers.

**Against the film record.** The costume in the four character images matches the press description of the film's primary costume: an iron mask, green hooded cape and tunic, two large brooches joined by a chain, plated chest, armoured shoulders and metal gloves, described as "basic Jack Kirby-esque" ([SlashFilm](https://www.slashfilm.com/2237350/disney-d23-doctor-doom-avengers-doomsday-second-costume-photos/)). The olive-yellow arm band observed in `doom-1.png` agrees with a report that the Comic-Con costume display showed a yellow band on the left arm ([Comic Basics](https://www.comicbasics.com/every-doctor-doom-costume-reveal-from-avengers-doomsday-so-far-and-what-it-could-mean/)). The reading of `doom-2.png` as a photograph of a displayed costume is consistent with the official costume exhibits at Comic-Con and D23, though the event behind this file is unidentified. The film's second, plainer costume is not represented in the library at all. The throne and tapestry frames match press descriptions of the trailer, and they sit awkwardly beside the lore researcher's conclusion that no described look at the film's castle interior exists; RESEARCH.md records that conflict under "Where the notes disagree".

**Against the clasp reports.** The image researcher saw a radiating star on one medallion and a square-and-cross on the other and recorded their meaning as unknown. Press coverage reads the film's clasps as Captain Marvel's emblem and a Mjolnir device and reports a fan theory that they are trophies, unconfirmed by Marvel ([SYFY WIRE](https://www.syfy.com/syfy-wire/doctor-doom-cloak-fasteners-in-avengers-doomsday-explored)). The descriptions are compatible. If they are the same thing, those two motifs are other characters' emblems, which makes them the least suitable ornament in the set to redraw. The tile, rivet, chain and beaded-border motifs carry no such doubt.

**Against the comics.** Nothing in the library is a comic-book image, and the film room as supplied shows no banner, flag or crest, while the comics' Latveria is full of them: a "D" cypher, an eagle, an eagle on a sceptre, a red flag with a black eagle ([Frogoat blog](https://frogoat.blogspot.com/search/label/flag)). The library also has no village, no robots, no gallery, laboratory or forge. The hidden-face images do match Kirby's stated idea that Doom is "the classic conception of Death" and that this "was the reason for the armor and the hood" ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Doctor_Doom)): `doom-1.png` is as close to a reaper as a monarch. The tapestry of a mother and child fits Wikipedia's description of the film's Doom as someone who has lost a child ([Wikipedia: Avengers: Doomsday](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Avengers:_Doomsday)), which is the source of the library's mournful register.

**Against fan and AI imagery.** Two of the seven files are probably derivative (`doom-3.png` and `doom-throme.jpg`), which is in line with the documented flood of fan and AI Doom imagery. The lore researcher's list of likely fan inventions includes cathedral-scale throne halls and default green glow; the library's two best images contain both, and press descriptions tie them to the official trailer. The practical reading is that the film uses these devices with restraint (one room, one small saturated accent), and that restraint, not their absence, is what separates the frames from generic fan art.

**Against the real throne rooms.** The film's throne room uses the devices the real-world research identified: a counted flight of about eight steps (Aachen has six, the Winter Palace seven), strict axial symmetry of the kind seen in Vladislav Hall, and a seat framed from behind. Where real throne rooms place heraldry or the ruler's image behind the throne, the film places a single gold filigree panel. The throne itself, a monolith with ornament in one place, is nearer to the unadorned marble seat at Aachen than to a gilded Baroque chair. The real-world researcher found no architectural source for light from behind a throne; the film frames are the project's only evidence for that device.

**Against the castles and the gothic question.** The library has no exterior, so the Carpathian and Bohemian castles in RESEARCH.md fill a complete gap, not a partial one. They also offer a way through the gothic conflict. Two of the region's most famous castles are medieval shells reworked by later hands (Corvin's "fanciful restoration", Karlštejn's 1887–1899 rebuilding), and Peleș shows what a Carpathian monarchy built for itself between 1873 and 1914, so a Gothic exterior with a later, more stylised interior is historically plausible for a Latverian castle. The film's plain, monumental piers also match the reductive approach the production-design interviews describe, "the idea of a castle" and "avoid fussiness", more than they match maximalist gothic.

**Against the manuscripts and metalwork.** The frames' balance of near-black, gold light and one green accent resembles the black books of hours, in particular the Morgan manuscript's gold initials on green grounds. The cast medallions' "aged pewter-silver finish and darkened recesses" agree with the real-world finding that aged metal is irregular and dark in its recesses. The circular floor glyph behaves like a seal, and real seals are circular with a legend around the rim.

**Against the design research.** The measured base blacks (#0B0F0C to #10110D) agree with the near-black backgrounds of all three proposed palette directions. The film's greens do not: they are olive and sage, yellow-leaning and muted, while the "Steel and emerald" direction proposes cleaner, bluer emeralds. Neither note tested its palette against the other's. The poster's extended technical sans-serif has no counterpart in any of the three proposed type systems.

**Against the motion research.** The film stages its host as a small, still, bowed figure, which agrees with the motion researcher's judgement that authority "reads as not needing to move". The light shafts and haze correspond to the low-cost atmospheric options (a slow fog layer, static painted rays with a faint drift). The glyph's green sparks conflict with the motion researcher's view that sparks and embers read as game key art.

## What the library lacks

The image researcher's inventory of absences is specific, and all of it is observed or measured. The `inspiration` folder is empty, so there are **no references for invitation design, interaction, motion, typography or layout**. There is **no exterior image** of a castle, town, landscape, sky or weather. There is **no heraldry**: no crest, flag, banner, seal or coat of arms is legible, and the medallions and floor plaques are too small to read. The only typography is one poster tagline and two studio logos. There are no texture or material close-ups apart from one small, colour-distorted file. No character image exceeds 872 pixels in width. There are no side or back views of the costume, no thresholds or approach imagery (gates, doors, corridors, bridges), no people other than the single figure, no classic or modern comic-book references, and no source, credit or licence information.

> **Inferred (image researcher).** Before design begins a team would want Latverian exteriors (castle silhouette, mountains, village, night and storm); a heraldic system; display and text typefaces suited to a royal summons; physical invitation artefacts (wax seals, parchment, engraved cards); clean, high-resolution material studies of brushed steel, verdigris bronze, wool weave, leaded glass and polished dark stone; motion references for haze, light shafts, cape and sparks; immersive web precedents; a few comic-era images, so that "recognisable Doom-inspired elements" are defined by what persists across interpretations and not by one film's costume; and a rights position for every reference.

RESEARCH.md supplies written and partly viewed research for most of these (castles, charters and seals, heraldry, manuscripts, typefaces, precedent sites), but it adds no images to the library, and its own access limits apply: no typeface and no website was seen.

Ranked by the image researcher against the stated direction (Latveria; royal, gothic, mysterious, imposing, cinematic; immersive; hybrid artwork). No stakeholder preference was available to test the ranking.

> **Inferred (image researcher).** None of the seven is a strong reference for "gothic" in the architectural sense or for "Latveria" as a place beyond one building's interior; the direction's first word is the least supported by the library. The two strongest anchors are both film frames, so a hybrid approach that used them directly would lean heavily on studio-owned imagery. Their safest use is as lighting, palette and composition guides for original environments. If the library is expanded, priority should go to what the anchors cannot supply: exteriors, heraldry, typography and high-resolution textures.

## Conclusion

The library's narrowness is a constraint and a clarification. It tells the next phase what the film's Doom looks like in one room under one light, and it does so consistently enough that a single frame can set the grade for everything original that follows. It also shows that the project's chosen hybrid is not a stylistic preference but a necessity: there is no supplied image of Latveria's exterior, state or documents to be faithful to, so those parts will be original whatever is decided.

The most useful single habit to carry forward from the images is their restraint. One saturated colour in a very small area, ornament in one place on the throne, one bowed and motionless figure in a vast dark room: the frames achieve "imposing" by withholding. CREATIVE_FINDINGS.md takes that as its starting point and sets out the choices that the images alone cannot make.
