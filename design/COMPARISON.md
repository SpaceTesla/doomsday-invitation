# Comparison of the three directions

Phase 1.5. This compares the three directions in `CREATIVE_DIRECTIONS.md` as they actually look on the boards in `explorations/`, and gives a recommendation. Open `explorations/index.html` to see them side by side.

**Recommendation in one line:** take Direction I, the Winter Chancery, as the spine; borrow the light and the council's room from Direction II; borrow the emerald ring and the build discipline from Direction III. Details and reasons are below. Nothing proceeds until this is approved.

## How to read the scores

The scores are my judgement from the rendered boards, on a scale of 1 (weak) to 5 (strong). They rest on static vector concept boards viewed in desktop Chrome. No board was tested on a real phone, no motion or sound exists yet, and the boards flatter the directions that need little artwork and undersell the one that needs a painted scene. Treat a one-point gap as no gap.

## Criterion by criterion

### Authenticity to Doctor Doom and Latveria

- **I (4).** The most Latverian: it is the only board that shows the country, and its chancery, seal and arms are built from what canon supplies (the eagle and the sceptre, the Carpathian setting, a 16th-century castle). It is the least Doom at a glance. He is present only as a name, a voice and a seal.
- **II (4).** The most faithful to the film's Doom: the bowed sovereign, the hall, the olive haze and amber light. It shows nothing of Latveria outside one room, and its accuracy depends on a film that is not out.
- **III (3).** The most Doom at a glance, and the only one that includes the scientist. It shows the least of Latveria, and its cold "you have been identified" register drops the courtliness and honour that the research names as his defining traits.

### Visual distinctiveness

- **I (5).** Silver script on black vellum with a gold initial on green is unlike any invitation or film site the research found. It has a real 15th-century precedent and no template.
- **II (2).** A throne room, a parchment, serif capitals and a gold seal. Each part is well made, and together they are what everyone expects. Of the three it sits nearest the luxury template and the royal-wedding invitation.
- **III (3).** Clearly not fantasy. But a hooded figure, a glowing ring and a monospaced line belong to a genre too. On the board it stays on the right side of the line; it would not take much to cross it.

### Cinematic potential

- **I (3).** Quiet by design: snow, one window, a seal that cracks. Its drama is in the page, not the scene. The flat castle illustration reads a little storybook, which a painted version would fix.
- **II (5).** One axis, crossing shafts, a figure who does not move. This is the direction that would look like a film still, and the one where sound would do the most.
- **III (4).** The ring drawn in light and the plate seam opening are strong single beats. Between them there is less to look at.

### Suitability for an invitation experience

- **I (5).** It is a letter. Receiving it, breaking the seal, reading your name and answering are the whole experience, and each is an act the guest already understands.
- **II (4).** Being received at court suits a summons, and "take your seat" lands naturally in a room. The open sheet has no seal to break, so the gate is an approach instead of an act.
- **III (2).** It reads as access control. Being recognised by a system is not being invited by a host, and for a movie night among friends it is the coldest of the three.

### Readability and usability

- **I (4).** Body text is 13.7:1 at a comfortable size, and the ruling helps the eye. It is still a long passage of light text on black, and gold on green in the initial is a display-only pairing.
- **II (5).** Ink on parchment at 12.7:1 is the easiest reading on any board. The headline sits cleanly in the dark floor band.
- **III (4).** High contrast and the most functional type, which would suit the council best. Bodoni's hairlines hold at the sizes shown and are untested smaller.

### Mobile adaptability

- **I (4).** The letter is portrait by nature and fits a phone unchanged. The castle needs its own phone composition, which the board shows working.
- **II (2).** The hall is a wide, symmetrical image. On the phone board the throne and glass survive but the figure shrinks to a few pixels and the room loses its width, which is most of its effect.
- **III (5).** A standing figure and an upright plate. Nothing needs recomposing.

### Technical feasibility

- **I (4).** Type, line and one illustration. The page is nearly finished as drawn. The castle wants better artwork, and snow is the only ambient effect.
- **II (2).** It needs a painted hall at two compositions, convincing light shafts and a material-quality seal to reach what the direction promises. That is the largest art and performance cost, and the result must hold on an older phone inside a chat app's browser.
- **III (5).** Lines, plates and one light. Everything on the board is already close to shippable vector.

## What the boards showed that the written directions did not

- **The black-vellum page is the best single thing made in this phase.** It reads as an object with a history, and it is fully legible. Neither the parchment nor the steel plate matches it.
- **Direction II's hall is the hardest to judge fairly.** Flat vector cannot show what a painted hall would. It scores low on feasibility for exactly the reason it would score highest if the artwork existed.
- **Direction III's figure is the most recognisable image and the most exposed.** It draws a hood, clasps, a chain and a tiled chest. It is original drawing with no face, but it is the closest any board comes to a Marvel-owned costume.
- **The typefaces behave.** IM Fell English has real ink character at headline size; Castoro Titling is elegant but anonymous; Bodoni Moda is striking and cold. Grenze Gotisch works as a single initial and would be too much as a word.
- **None of the three shows Doom's humour.** All are played straight.

## Recommendation: Direction I as the spine, with two borrowings

I recommend a hybrid, and I recommend it as one identity with a clear rule for what comes from where, so it does not become three styles in sequence.

**The rule:** the guest holds a letter (Direction I), is led from the cold outside into a warm room (the light of Direction II), and the kingdom's hidden machinery shows exactly once, as light (the ring of Direction III).

### What comes from each direction

| From | What is taken | Where it is used |
|---|---|---|
| **I. Winter Chancery** | The letter close on black vellum: versal on green, silver text on gold ruling, the guest's name in gold, the sanction in rubric red | The summons; the voice and layout of everything Doom "writes", including his proposals to the council |
| **I** | The green wax seal and the arms of Latveria (eagle and sceptre), at two levels of detail | The gate (break the seal), the approval gesture (press the seal), the small mark everywhere |
| **I** | The type system: IM Fell English, EB Garamond, one Grenze Gotisch initial, a quiet sans for controls | Everywhere |
| **I** | The night valley and the single lit window | The opening and the link preview |
| **II. The Audience** | The temperature rule: cold blue-black outside, warm gold light inside, warm always meaning "toward the guest" | The transition from summons to council |
| **II** | The hall: one axis, counted steps, crossing light, a small still figure | The council's setting, and the only place Doom appears |
| **II** | Stillness as authority | Doom never moves; motion belongs to the guest's acts |
| **III. Iron Protocol** | The ring of emerald light | The council's tally: one segment lights for each approval and stays lit. It is the only saturated green and the only visible technology in the experience |
| **III** | Portrait first, and built from type, line and light | Every scene is composed for the phone before the desktop; nothing on the core path waits on a painted asset |
| **III** | Steel as the neutral | Labels, borders and controls in the council take the grey-silver range, so the interface recedes behind the documents |

### What is left out, and why

- **The parchment sheet and gilt seal (II).** Legible, but the most conventional object on any board.
- **The steel plate, the machine voice and Bodoni (III).** Strong, but a second document style would compete with the letter, and the monospaced line is the element nearest to a gaming cliché.
- **The hooded figure as the opening image (III).** It is the image closest to studio-owned design, and the research favours Doom arriving through his document and voice.
- **"Doom has identified you" (III).** The summons should be an honour that cannot be declined, not a detection.

### Why this holds together as one identity

- **One document style.** Everything Doom issues looks like the letter. The council's proposals are short decrees in the same hand.
- **One mark.** The eagle seal appears at every scale. The ring is not a second logo; it is the seal's rim, lit.
- **One colour logic.** Black base, silver for words, gold for the guest's name and for light, green as wax, red for consequence. Emerald light appears only on the ring.
- **One journey.** Outside to inside, cold to warm, sealed to open. That is also the order of the five motion beats the research identified.

### What it costs

- It gives up the most cinematic opening. If the first five seconds must look like a film, Direction II is the answer and the hall needs commissioning.
- It keeps one blackletter glyph, with the association the research warns about. Replacing the versal with an IM Fell capital is a small change if that is uncomfortable.
- The hall still has to be made well enough to serve as the council's backdrop, though as a dimmed setting behind an interface it needs far less than it would as a hero.
- Doom is not seen until the council. Guests who expect the mask up front will not get it.

## Decisions I need from you

1. **Approve the hybrid, or choose a single direction.** If a single one, I would choose I.
2. **The opening.** The valley and castle as on the Direction I board, or the sealed letter alone emerging from black, with the castle held back for the link preview. The research favours the second; the board shows the first.
3. **Whether Doom appears.** Small and still in the council hall only (my recommendation), also as the hooded figure somewhere earlier, or never.
4. **The blackletter initial.** Keep it, or replace it with a Roman capital.
5. **The world's black.** Direction I's cool blue-black throughout, or shifting to the film's olive-black once inside the hall (my recommendation, as part of the temperature rule).
6. **Tone.** Played straight as on the boards, or with some of the dry humour Marvel's own material gives Doom's hospitality.

Once these are settled the next step would be a fourth board showing the hybrid as one piece, before any design-language document is written. I have not started it.
