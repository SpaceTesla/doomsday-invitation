IV. The Iron Throne

Science, sorcery and sovereignty in one object: a ring of three bands that is a rose window, a lock and a seal. The hall and its light come from Direction II, the steel and its engraving from Direction III. Doom is now drawn as Doom, and the runes are traced from the decoder you supplied.

A. The collection

The opening. The screen cuts to black, a ring of runes lights under the guest, and a door stands open onto the hall with Doom already in it. He says two things. Only the name is large.

Taken from the Banner scene

  • The lights go out. A hard cut to black, not a fade.
  • The floor lights. Green symbols appear underfoot before anything else does.
  • He is already there. No entrance and no walk.
  • A name, then a purpose. Two short lines and nothing after them.

Added by us

  • The door. A lancet standing open in the guest's own dark, with the hall behind it. The scene as reported has no door.
  • Doom is seen. Reports describe a voice in the dark. Here he stands backlit, with the ring's light on the underside of the mask.
  • The answer. The guest goes with him by choice.

Still open

  • The title. "Mr." needs a per-guest field, or a fallback of the name alone.
  • The quoted line. One published account gives it as "Dr. Banner, I'm here to collect you." I kept your wording.
  • The voice. The lines are set as type. No voice is imitated.

B. The throne hall

The council chamber. Gothic lancets and clustered columns on one axis, gold light from the glass, and the three-band ring set in the wall where a rose window would be. Emerald appears in two places only: the ring above the throne and the ring in the floor.

How the room is his

  • The rose window is a machine. Gold band, steel scale, emerald runes: the same ring as his seal.
  • The throne carries the mark. The ring appears once more, small, above his head.
  • The floor is inscribed. The ring that lit under the guest in the opening is inlaid here.

Light

  • Gold is the room's light. It comes from glass and falls in shafts.
  • Emerald is his. It comes only from runes that are active.
  • Half the frame stays near black. The columns are read by their lit edges.

What this drawing is

  • A vector stand-in that fixes the layout, the palette and where the light falls.
  • It is not film-quality artwork. A painted or rendered hall would replace it.

C. The royal summons

A steel plate, issued and sealed. The wording keeps the order of a royal charter; the object is engineered. The four phrases engraved round its edge are the four that have been read on the film's own material.

Where the runes live

Rules for using it

  • Inscription only. Runes are never the only carrier of something the guest must read.
  • Fixed phrases. Guest names stay in Latin letters. Many names cannot be spelled, and half-spelled names would be invented.
  • Light means active. A rune is engraved until a mechanism turns it on.
  • No other symbols. No Norse runes, no alchemical signs, no circuit traces.

What is still a guess

  • Whether one rune per letter is right. Fans describe the script as partly phonetic: the coffee packaging spells its word "kavi".
  • Whether the film's script has word dividers. The dot between words on the rings is our addition.
  • Which pocket of the jacket reads which word. Two published accounts disagree.

Not available to me

  • The two Reddit threads could not be opened from here. The facts above come from the decoder image and from news coverage of the same decoding.
  • I have not seen the jacket, the packaging or the film scene, only descriptions of them.

F. Motion and atmosphere

Six beats, drawn as still frames. Nothing here moves yet. Mechanisms turn and lock; light switches on; Doom never moves at all.

Rules of motion

  • Machines turn, light switches. Rotation, parting and locking for objects. On and off for light. Nothing floats, bounces or drifts in.
  • He is still. Doom is never animated. The camera and the light move around him.
  • One ambient movement. Slow haze in the gold shafts. No particles and no flicker.
  • Reduced motion. Every beat becomes a 0.2 s cross-fade to its end state.

Sound, proposed

  • Lights out. The room tone stops dead and leaves silence.
  • The ring. A low electrical hum that rises as the runes light.
  • Runes and bands. Dry metal ticks, one per rune; a heavier detent as each band locks.
  • The door and the plate. Stone on stone, short and low. Steel parting on a seam.
  • The hall. A large quiet room: long reverb, distant wind.

Limits on sound

  • Nothing plays before the guest's first tap. "Go without sound" is always offered.
  • No music bed and no voice. His lines appear as type.
  • Every sound is under two seconds except the hum and the room.

Against Directions II and III

What this board keeps, what it changes and what it drops.

Retained

  • From II: the hall on one axis, seven gilded steps, gold light through tall glass, a figure who does not move.
  • From III: the steel plate, the dotted border, the emerald ring with the nested square, silver type, Bodoni for names.
  • From I: the charter order of the wording, and the idea that the summons is one sealed object.

Improved

  • Doom. II drew a small hooded shape and III a faceless one. This is the mask, the hood, the clasps and chain, and the tiled cuirass.
  • The hall. Columns in depth, lancets, a floor that reflects, shafts of light. II's hall was a flat elevation.
  • The ring. III's ring carried a Latin legend in monospace. It now has three bands with a meaning each, and it is also the room's window.
  • The plate. Brushed grain, engraved runes on all four edges, corner bolts, and the date under the seal.
  • The opening. III said "Doom has identified you". This addresses the guest by name and tells them why he has come.

Discarded

  • II's parchment and gilt seal. The summons is steel.
  • III's monospace line and coordinates. They read as a game interface.
  • III's hold-to-be-recognised gate. It read as a security scan.
  • The eagle arms and the wax seal of I. The ring and the mask are the heraldry now.
  • Castoro Titling, Cormorant, Crimson Pro. One display face and one text face remain.

What this board cannot show

It is vector drawing

  • No image generator was available, so every scene is original vector drawing. The materials are approximations made with noise and gradients.
  • The figure is recognisable but stiff. Final artwork should replace it.

It does not move

  • Motion and sound are described and storyboarded only.
  • The phone frames are simulated in a desktop browser and were not checked on a device.

It is close to Marvel's design by request

  • The mask, hood, clasps and cuirass follow the film costume. The clasp emblems are replaced with the nested square.
  • Fine for a private invitation among friends. It would need a second look before anything public.
