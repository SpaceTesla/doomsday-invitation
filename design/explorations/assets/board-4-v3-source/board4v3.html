A. The updated hero

The approved opening with the corrected figure in it. The fog, the rubble, the light, the position of the hand and of the message, and the type are the same files and settings as before. Only the two figure layers were rendered again.

Taken from the three reveal frames

  • The order of disclosure. Hand, then torso, then head. The first frame shows only a hand in a gap of light; the second hides the head behind the slab; the third shows the hood.
  • A soft foreground. The rubble is close to the lens and out of focus. It frames the figure and never shows detail.
  • One source of light. Pale fog lit from behind, brightest behind the hand. The figure is read by its outline.
  • Ash in the air. Sparse streaks falling across the light, and large soft discs low in the frame.

Changed for the invitation

  • The point of view. The guest looks up from under the stone. "Here to collect you" then has a literal meaning.
  • A fourth step. The film frames end on a silhouette. Here the costume then comes up out of the dark: hood edge, half the mask, the clasps.
  • The rune on his arm. The suit rune on the vambrace lights emerald while the stone lifts. It is the only emerald in the scene.
  • The grade. The fog is tinted towards the gold-green of the hall's glass so the two scenes belong together.

Layout rules

  • Wide screens. Scene fills the viewport. Name, line and actions are centred in the lowest third. Nothing sits in a left column.
  • Phones. Figure in the upper 60 percent, message and actions below it on the rubble. The primary action stays within thumb reach.
  • Between the two. The figure layer keeps its height and the rubble layers slide inwards, so the frame never shows an empty side.

What changed

  • The crown of the hood came down by nearly half, and its point is rounder.
  • The sides of the hood are thinner beside the face and widen below the cheek, so the cloth falls onto the shoulders. Before, it pinched in at a neck.
  • The clasps and chain moved up to the foot of the hood and closer together. The cuirass starts higher to meet them.
  • The shoulders are narrower and slope from the neck.
  • The whole figure is drawn about 7 percent larger. The slimmer hood and shoulders leave it the same space in the frame, and the mask gains the difference.

What did not change

  • The mask. The same measured master, at the same detail. It is only shown larger.
  • Light and material. The same lamps, the same wool and steel, the same shadow under the hood.
  • The frame. The clasps, the hand and the message are where they were, to within a few pixels.

Where the references disagree

  • The hood in doom-3 has a taller peak (0.54) than in doom-2 and doom-4 (0.31 to 0.38). The figure sits between them.
  • The hand reference is a collectable figure in the comic costume. Its hood and shoulders agree with the film images, so it is used for those too.

Anatomy

  • Fingers have three joints each. Each segment tapers, each knuckle is a little fuller, and each finger turns slightly in towards the middle of the hand.
  • Lengths follow a hand. Middle longest, ring and index close behind, little finger a quarter shorter. The fingers meet at their bases.
  • The palm is a palm. Fuller at the base of the thumb and along the outer edge, hollow in the middle, with its two main creases.
  • The thumb starts low on the palm and has two joints.

Connection

  • A wrist narrower than the palm, then two bands, then the cuff.
  • The cuff flares away from the hand and overlaps the forearm plate, as on the reference.
  • The forearm rises from an elbow under the shoulder. The cloak lies over the upper arm and hangs behind the forearm.
  • Round sections. Forearm, cuff and wrist are shaded as turned forms.

Kept, and still short

  • The gesture. The reference shows a clawed hand from the back, on the opposite arm. Ours stays an open palm on the same side as before. Nothing was mirrored.
  • The lighting. The hand is still a dark shape against the lamp, with a little more of its form showing.
  • Plates. The palm side of a gauntlet is mostly leather, so the finger plates of the reference do not show from this view.
  • Still a render. It reads as a connected, proportioned hand. It is short of a painted or modelled one.

C. Typography

Seventeen faces set in the same five lines, three complete combinations, and one recommendation. Nothing approved has been switched: the hero, the summons and the controls on this board still use the approved faces. The recommended set is shown applied in two small frames at the end of this section.

The two models

  • The Doom lettering. doom-logo-text.png: a narrow lowercase blackletter with a diamond set in each o.
  • The Doomsday line. No file of the Avengers: Doomsday logo is in the reference library. The one sample available is the date line at the foot of doom-1.png: wide, evenly spaced geometric capitals.

How to read the cards

  • The five lines are set in capitals, as given. Under them the three names are repeated in mixed case.
  • Each card gives the licence and the weights the family actually has, read from its entry in the Google Fonts source.

The finding that decides most of it

  • Blackletter capitals were never meant to be set together. Every true blackletter here fails on "THE IRON THRONE" in capitals and passes in mixed case, so the display face is used in mixed case only.

Recommendation: combination one

Why

  • It adds the two things asked for and removes nothing approved. Guest names stay in Bodoni Moda and the summons stays in Spectral.
  • Grenze Gotisch is the one gothic face that stays readable. It has the faceted o and broken strokes of the Doom lettering on letterforms a guest can read at once.
  • Oxanium gives the technical note without the costume. Squared like Orbitron, but compact, with ordinary numerals and a usable lowercase.
  • The page stays regal. By area it is still almost all serif. The technical face appears on controls and counts, the gothic face on a handful of titles.

Where each face goes

  • Grenze Gotisch 500. "The Iron Throne", "Council of Doom", and Doom's own name where he issues something. Mixed case, 22 px and up. Never a guest's name.
  • Bodoni Moda 500, italic 400. Guest names and the lines Doom speaks.
  • Oxanium 600, capitals, spaced. Buttons, labels, council status. Oxanium 500 in mixed case for small interface text.
  • Spectral. The summons and everything read at length.

What it would change

  • IBM Plex Sans is replaced by Oxanium on controls and labels.
  • "Victor von Doom" on the summons moves from Bodoni Moda to Grenze Gotisch. This is the one visible change to an approved frame, and it is yours to refuse.
  • Orbitron is not used. Its numerals and its width cost more than its character gives. It is in combination two if you want to see it in place.

D. Animation storyboard

Four beats, built from the same eleven layers as the frames above. The first row is the supplied reference sequence; the second is ours. Below them the sequence can be played.

Reference: the supplied frames, in order

Reference stills from the film, supplied in references/inspiration/. Shown for comparison only.

Ours

Play it

How it is built

  • Eleven flat layers. Fog, a glow behind the hand, the figure twice (silhouette and lit), low haze, ash, lens discs, and four pieces of rubble.
  • Movement is translation. Each piece of rubble slides and turns by a degree or two. The figure and fog ease from 104 percent scale to 100, which reads as the camera settling.
  • Light is a cross-fade. The lit figure fades in over the silhouette. No filter is animated.
  • No 3D and no physics. The rubble is four images. Dust is one tiled image moving on a loop.

For the build, later

  • One timeline. A single GSAP timeline with five labels: dark, lift, clear, lit, summons. The sketch here uses plain CSS with timers.
  • Pointer parallax. Rubble moves about 12 px against the pointer, the figure about 4 px, the fog not at all. Off on touch screens.
  • Masked text. Each line is revealed by a clip that opens left to right in about 0.6 s. The letters themselves do not move.
  • Weight. The desktop layers total about 0.4 MB as WebP and JPEG, and the phone set about the same. Rubble is stored at half size because it is out of focus.

Reduced motion and fallbacks

  • Reduced motion. The final frame fades in over 0.2 s with the text already set. No drift, no ash, no parallax.
  • Skipping. Any key or tap during the sequence jumps to the final frame. The actions are in the page from the start for assistive technology.
  • Return visits. The sequence plays once per guest; later visits open on the final frame.
  • Slow connections. Fog and silhouette load first and are enough for beats 1 and 2. The lit figure can arrive during beat 2.

E. The throne hall

Rebuilt on the plan of the supplied wide shot. The Gothic lancets, clustered columns and rose window of version one are gone, because the reference has none of them.

Against the reference

Kept from the reference

  • The axis. Throne, pier and slit on the centre line, everything else mirrored about it.
  • The glass. Uprights that bend and part like stems, tied by short level bars. Pale yellow at mid height, green and dirtier near the floor.
  • The fins. Dark slanted masses that widen as they climb, with more glass behind them.
  • Amber in narrow strips. In the pillars and in the slit, and nowhere else.
  • The dais. Nine shallow steps, canted at the sides, with a line of gold on the middle of each edge.
  • The floor. Dark, polished, with a large inlaid circle that burns green along some of its lines.
  • The lamps. Pale bowls on dark posts at the side walls.

Ours

  • The circle carries Doomscript. "There is no promise of tomorro", the phrase the first board used for its floor ring.
  • The lattice inside it. The hexagon and centre square of the suit rune, in place of the reference's star.
  • The panel above his head. The suit rune in gold. The reference has an ornament there that cannot be read at this size.
  • The seal has moved. Version one set the three-band seal in the wall as a rose window. It now belongs to the summons and to the scene change only.

Light and colour

  • Direction. Sun high on the right. Shafts cross the right-hand bay and the steps.
  • Three colours of light. Yellow-green from the glass, amber from the strips, emerald from the floor.
  • Depth. The glass is far brighter than the structure, so it blooms over the leading. The floor mirrors the wall and fades with distance.
  • Where it falls short. The structure is flat elevation with shading. It has no carved detail, and the fins have no thickness.

F. The canonical mask

One set of outlines, measured from the supplied mask image, is the source for every mask on this board. The three levels below are the same coordinates with detail removed or relief added. The mask image is in references/inspiration/, where it was found by looking; the brief placed it under character/.

What was measured

FeatureMeasurement, in units of one hundredth of the width
Proportion100 wide, 150 tall. Widest at the eye line, exactly half way down.
OutlineA dome to the temples, fullest at the cheek (y 73 to 80), a step in of about 2 units at y 96 where the jaw plates begin, then a taper to a flat chin about 24 wide.
CrownA hasp 10 wide on the centre line carrying the horned mark. A linked edging from temple to temple, ending in a tab at each side.
BrowTwo plates, each from x 2 to 40 and y 35 to 74. Their inner edges sweep down in a concave curve and stop 4 units apart at y 61. A lid plate is riveted over the lower third: 10 rivets each side. Five more rivets each side form a V on the forehead.
EyesEach about 27 wide and 16 tall. The top edge falls 7 units towards the nose; the bottom edge is level. Inner corners 15 apart.
NoseA wedge from 5.6 wide at the bridge (y 74) to 21 at its base (y 97), with a flat ridge about 4 wide, ending at y 103.
CheeksA plate under each eye with 12 rivets along its upper edge. A deep crease runs from the flank of the nose down and out to the jaw.
MouthA slot 33 wide with splayed ends, crossed by a bar with a centre block. Under it a lip bar whose ends turn down, repeated by the line of the chin plate.
Jaw and chinSide plates with a double strap line, 7 rivets inboard and 11 along the edge. A rounded boss 29 wide on the chin.

How the three levels stay one mask

  • Same coordinates. Level 1, level 2 and the relief are generated from one file of points. None of them is redrawn by hand.
  • What every level keeps. The outline with its step at the jaw, the falling top edge of the eyes, the splayed mouth, and the two cheek creases. These four are what make it this mask.
  • Level 1 drops. Rivets, plates, nose and chin. The eye openings use the frame line, 1.7 units larger, so they hold at 16 px.
  • Level 2 adds. Brow and lid plates, nose with its ridge, lip and chin lines, jaw straps, and all 90 rivets as dots. The linked edging becomes a dotted line, which is the border the plates already use.
  • Level 3 adds. Height, the wrap of the face, worked-steel surface, grime in the recesses and a cast shadow from each plate.

Where the master departs from the reference

  • The crown edging has 13 links a side; the reference count could not be read exactly.
  • The mouth grille is reduced to a bar, a block and two posts.
  • The base of the nose is rounder than the reference, which has a crisper lower edge.
  • The reference is lit from the upper left, so the far right edge is partly inferred by mirroring.

In use

G. The suit rune

The mark raised on the armour in the two rune views. Both views are small, angled and partly in shadow, so this is a reading. The parts that are certain and the part that is inferred are drawn differently on the master.

What can be read

  • A hexagon with a doubled outline, standing on a point.
  • Three lines through the centre joining opposite corners, and a fourth joining the middles of the two upright sides.
  • A square on its corner at the centre, half the radius across each way.
  • Brackets outside the hexagon: two strokes that leave at 45 degrees and turn back towards each other without meeting.

What is uncertain

  • The fourth bracket. View 2 has no room for it above the hexagon. View 1 seems to show four. The master draws it dashed; levels 1 and 2 include it for symmetry.
  • Small strokes near the centre. View 2 has what may be a second, smaller square. It is left out.
  • Where on the suit it sits. Neither view shows enough of the armour to say. The figure carries it on the forearm plate, which is our choice.
  • Its meaning. Unknown. It is used as a mark and never as a letter.

Where it is used

  • On the vambrace in the opening, lit emerald while the stone lifts.
  • In gold on the panel above the throne.
  • As the lattice inside the floor circle.
  • At level 1 as the icon for the roster of guests.

What makes it one family

  • Straight strokes and one circle. The mask is the only form with curves, which keeps it the most important mark.
  • Two weights. A heavy outline and a light inner line, in the ratio the plate border uses.
  • Dots. Rivets on the mask, the dotted border on the plates and the ticks on the seal are the same device.

L. Kept, changed and unfinished

Kept as approved

  • Doomscript: the traced runes, the decoder sheet, the inscription table and the rules for use.
  • The three-band seal and its mechanism.
  • The steel summons: plate, dotted border, engraved edges, bolts, wording.
  • Type: Bodoni Moda, Spectral, IBM Plex Sans.
  • The emerald and antique-gold palette and every interface colour.
  • Controls, panels and their states.
  • The two lines of the opening and the two actions.

Changed

  • The opening. A doorway with text on the left became a reveal through rubble with the message at the foot.
  • Doom. Flat vector shapes became a lit relief. The mask follows the measured master.
  • The hall. Lancets, columns and rose window became the pier, bays and fins of the reference.
  • Mask icons. One master and three levels replace the earlier sketch.
  • The suit rune. New.
  • Rules of motion. Version one said nothing drifts and Doom never moves. Ash now drifts in the opening, and the rubble moves. Doom himself still does not.
  • In this revision. The proportions of the hood, shoulders and clasps, and the anatomy of the raised hand and arm. A typography recommendation is added and not yet applied.

Still a placeholder

  • The figure. A procedural render. Its proportions and its hand now follow the references. The cloth still has few folds and no visible weave. It is short of film quality.
  • The hall. A shaded elevation without carved detail. The seated figure is a small stand-in.
  • The rubble. Soft dark shapes. They work because they are out of focus.
  • Motion. A sketch in CSS, checked in a desktop browser. Pointer parallax, masked text and the skip control are described but not built.
  • Sound. Described on the first board only.
  • Phone frames. Simulated at 390 by 844 in a desktop browser.

Finished enough to carry forward

  • The mask master, with level 1 and level 2 as vector files.
  • The suit rune master and its two vector levels, subject to the open question on the fourth bracket.
  • The composition and timing of the reveal.
  • The plan and colour of the hall.

How the artwork was made

  • No image generator was available. Every scene is drawn as outlines in code, raised into a height map and lit by calculation.
  • No reference image is traced into the artwork or copied into the layers. The references appear on this board only where labelled, as links to the files in your library.
  • The generator is saved beside the assets so any layer can be re-rendered.

Close to Marvel's design by request

  • The mask, costume and hall now follow the film's designs more closely than version one did.
  • That suits a private invitation among friends. Anything public would need a second look first.
